_A Forth that started as WF32, spent a while trapped inside an LLVM macro
assembler, and finally broke free onto its own._

## TL;DR

- **What:** an Apple Silicon token-IR optimizing **Forth**, based on the WF32 STC
  heritage (Alex McDonald's Win32 Forth, adopted as the seed), **LLVM-free** with
  its own macro-assembler.
- **The arc:** WF32 STC → a Windows 64-bit port → embedded inside an LLVM macro
  assembler (huge overhead) → broke free of LLVM → its own assembler. Still,
  underneath, a Forth inside a macro assembler.
- **Compiler:** a simple tokenizing optimizer that grew until it *was* the Forth
  compiler, not just a phase over one.
- **Get it:** [Downloads](#download-run) · [Source](https://github.com/albanread/MF66)

## Where it sits

MF66 carries [WF66](/posts/wf66)'s token-IR compiler onto Apple Silicon, and is
itself the base of [MF67](/posts/mf67) ("Objective Forth"). See the
[timeline](/timeline).

## What it is

> _From the README —_ "I always liked the WF32 STC, and this is based on that
> original code, although it has gone through a series of transformations… For a
> while WF64 was embedded inside an LLVM macro assembler, which carries a huge
> overhead; eventually we broke free of LLVM, and now we use our own
> macro-assembler."

## Why I built it

- _Shedding LLVM's overhead for a lean, self-contained arm64 Forth._
- _Letting the tokenizing optimizer become the compiler outright._
